What to eat

The plates to plan a day around

Eight plates to plan a day around, and six snacks Lagos eats standing up — with somewhere to have each one.

Snacks

Between meals is when Lagos eats best. Puff-puff from the fryer, meat pie from the bakery shelf, chin chin in the glovebox.

01

Puff-puff

Fried dough balls, crisp at the edge and airy inside. A bag costs less than the taxi you are waiting for.

Where to eat in Lagos →

Roadside fryers from morning; every buka keeps a tray.

Recipe & photo: Joyful Cook ↗

02

Akara

Whipped beans fried into fritters, eaten hot with bread or pap. Best before ten in the morning.

Where to eat in Lagos →

Breakfast stalls and street corners, mornings only.

Recipe & photo: Joyful Cook ↗

03

Meat pie

Shortcrust pastry around minced beef, potato and carrot. The snack you buy for a journey and finish before the bridge.

Where to eat in Lagos →

Bakeries, kiosks and filling-station counters.

Recipe & photo: Joyful Cook ↗

04

Egg roll

A boiled egg wrapped in sweet dough and fried. Meat pie's quieter sibling, and the better breakfast.

Where to eat in Lagos →

The same bakery shelves as meat pie, usually beside them.

Recipe & photo: Joyful Cook ↗

05

Buns

Denser and sweeter than puff-puff, with a cracked top. Order them hot, when the oil is still going.

Where to eat in Lagos →

Fried to order at street corners and school gates.

Recipe & photo: Joyful Cook ↗

06

Chin chin

Sweet fried dough cut into cubes, crunchy enough to survive a car boot. Buy a tub, not a bag.

Where to eat in Lagos →

Sold in bags and tubs everywhere; good for the drive home.

Getting around

How Lagos actually moves

No longer operating

Uber stopped operating in Nigeria on 2 September 2026, and its keke tier went with it. Bolt, inDrive and LagRide below are the replacements.

Journey recipes

The three trips visitors actually make

Murtala Muhammed (LOS) → VI / Lekki

₦10,000–45,000

45 min–2.5 hrs

No train reaches the airport. With luggage, a car is the only sane first move.

  1. 1

    Arrivals

    —

    Clear immigration and baggage — 30 to 90 minutes is normal on an international arrival. Buy a SIM or get data before you book anything.

  2. 2

    Bolt or inDrive

    ₦10,000–25,000

    Pick the app pickup point, not the tout at the door. Fares surge when several wide-body arrivals land together.

  3. 3

    Pre-booked car

    ₦25,000–45,000

    The reliable version: fixed price, driver waits with your name, no negotiation at midnight.

Official airport taxis run ₦20,000–40,000 with no meter — agree before you get in. The budget chain (keke to Ikeja, Red Line to Oyingbo, Bolt to VI ≈ ₦4,500) exists, but it is weekday-only and miserable with bags.

Ikeja → Victoria Island / Ikoyi

₦3,500–12,000

60–120 min

The Red Line does the painful half for ₦1,000, then you finish by road.

  1. 1

    Red Line

    ₦1,000 full trip; less by zone

    Ikeja to Oyingbo, about 30 minutes on the shared corridor. Cowry card only, and it runs weekdays only — nine trains a day, so check the timetable before you commit.

  2. 2

    Bolt or keke

    ₦2,500–5,000

    Oyingbo to VI or Ikoyi. Oyingbo is a market and bus terminal, not Marina — agree the pickup pin before you walk out.

There is no interchange between the Red Line at Oyingbo and the Blue Line at Marina — the gap is a road journey that can eat the time you saved. Straight Bolt is ₦6,000–12,000 and 45–120 minutes depending on Third Mainland.

Marina / TBS → Mile 2 / Ikorodu

₦750–2,200

25–70 min

The Blue Line is the only trip in Lagos where traffic cannot touch you.

  1. 1

    Blue Line

    ₦750 full line

    Marina to Mile 2 in about 25 minutes. Trains every 8–12 minutes at peak, service to about 21:50, Cowry card only. Off-peak (10am–4pm) fares are discounted.

  2. 2

    BRT from Mile 2

    ₦680–960

    Mile 2 to TBS for the Island side, or pick up the Ikorodu corridor from TBS.

  3. 3

    Ferry alternative

    ₦2,200

    CMS/Marina to Ikorodu by water, roughly 50 minutes. Weekday departures cluster in the morning and late afternoon — miss the last one and you are on the road.

Blue Line runs Monday to Saturday with a reduced Sunday service; the Red Line does not run at weekends at all. Neither takes cash.

Every mode, one by one

01

Danfo

Yellow minibus

₦300–₦1,000

Cash, or transfer to the conductor

Flag it, shout your stop, squeeze in. The conductor collects.

Agree the fare before you sit down, and keep small notes. Handing over ₦5,000 for a ₦700 trip is how arguments start.

02

Keke napep

Tricycle

₦200–₦700

Cash

Short hops and estate roads. Ubiquitous off the main highways.

Restricted on many major roads. If a keke driver refuses a route, it is usually the law, not the traffic.

03

BRT

Regulated bus

₦450–₦1,080

Cowry card only

Dedicated lanes on the big corridors. Tap in at the terminal.

Since August 2026 cash is not allowed on LAMATA buses — top up Cowry before you leave the house.

04

Blue Line

Rail

₦750 full line

Cowry card

Marina to Mile 2 in about 25 minutes, no traffic.

The only reliable way to beat a Third Mainland jam. Off-peak fares are sometimes discounted.

05

Bolt

Ride-hailing

App quote

Card or wallet in app

The default app in Lagos, and the widest coverage.

Check the plate and driver name before you get in, and share the trip at night.

06

inDrive

Ride-hailing, bid a fare

Usually below Bolt

Card or cash

Name your price; drivers accept or counter. Also runs a Keke tier for 6–7km trips.

Cheaper if you are willing to haggle, slower if you are not.

07

LagRide

Lagos state e-taxi

App quote

Card or wallet

Government-backed fleet, petrol and electric, Lagos only.

Smaller fleet than Bolt, so it is a backup rather than a plan.

08

Ferry

LAGFERRY

₦2,200 Ikorodu–Falomo

Cash, POS or Cowry

Ikorodu–Falomo–CMS, Badore–Ikoyi. Weekdays, roughly 6:30am–6pm; last departures mid-afternoon.

Life jackets are mandatory and enforced. Shorter crossings cost less than the Ikorodu run, and the lagoon beats the road when the bridge is standing still.

Fares and journey estimates last checked 16 November 2026. Street fares move with fuel, rain and traffic, and app quotes surge in both — confirm before you pay.
